WWren HallowayThe Lamp at Gull's Reach · reply 212 · swipe 1 / 3

The lamp room smelled of paraffin and wet wool. Wren didn't look up from the lens.

“You're dripping on my floor,” she said. “Again.”

“It's raining,” Ash said, “in case the lighthouse keeper hadn't noticed.”

Below, the harbour bell clanged twice. Mr. Pell, closing early; his voice came up the stairwell thin with wind: “Halloway! Boat's not coming tonight. Tell your lodger!”

Wren's hand stilled on the brass. “Not my lodger,” she muttered, then louder, “We heard you!”

On the sill, a folded square of paper had been weighted down with an egg. Nan's hand, all loops: Eggs on the step. Don't let her skip supper.

“She writes to you,” Ash said.

“She writes to everyone.” Wren polished the same spot of glass a third time. “Tam used to say she'd write to the sea if it had a door.”
