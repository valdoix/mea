The four rooms, again

Another version of each room from the first sheet. Cozy goes pastel, the arcade goes neon and stays a bar more than a game, maximalist grows up, and elegant gets sharper. Same Lantern shell; flip any of them between day and night with the switch on its plaque.

I.

Meringue cozy, in pastels

The Hearthside idea in sugared almond colours: a tearoom on a rainy morning. The sidebar is a puffy patchwork quilt with tufted buttons, portraits sit in paper doilies, cards are held down with washi tape, bunting hangs across the top, and the typing dots are macarons. Day: Buttercream, strawberry milk and mint. Night: Moonmilk, lavender dusk, the bunting turns to fairy lights.

II.

Neon Moth arcade, in neon

An arcade bar at two in the morning rather than a game screen. No health bars, no HUD: just glass-tube signage, cards outlined like neon, a jukebox in the sidebar and a row of cabinets glowing at the back of the room. Pink for them, cyan for you, lime and violet for trim. Day: Matinee, the tubes as coloured glass on white tile. Night: Blacklight, everything lit.

III.

Menagerie maximalist, grown up

Jubilee's more-is-more, but for adults: a collector's palazzo instead of a party. Chintz wallpaper with a salon hang of gilt frames, a damask velvet sidebar behind gilded moulding, chats as framed specimen labels, a velvet pelmet with tassels, a kilim underfoot, and every bubble a different material (Greek key, Regency stripe, emerald velvet). Send is a wax seal. No stickers, no tilts, no emoji. Day: Palazzo. Night: Nocturne, aubergine and candlelight.

IV.

Maison elegant, stylish

Where Atelier is a literary journal, Maison is a fashion editorial: a couture house the night before the show. Thin Didone display at a large size against a light geometric sans, a black-and-white portrait plate with a look number, a credits line, lots of white space, and lines worth keeping set as pull quotes. One accent, camel by day and champagne by night. Day: Ivory. Night: Soirée.
