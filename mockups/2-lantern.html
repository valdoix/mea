PreviouslyThe ferry stopped after the equinox gale. Ash promised to find lamp oil before the long dark, and Wren pretended not to care whether he came back.
Wren

The lamp room hums as the great lens turns, throwing its slow gold arm across the black water. “You came back,” she says, not looking up from the brass. “Most people only climb those stairs once.”

He's soaked through. Again. Of course he is.

Ash

I shake the rain off my coat and set the oilskin parcel on her workbench. “Your oil. The ferryman wouldn't come past the rocks, so I rowed it.”

Wren ‹ 2 / 3 ›

Her hands go still on the cloth. “You rowed. In this.” She turns, and the lamplight catches the salt drying in her hair. “That's either very brave or very stupid, and I haven't decided which I'd prefer.”

The kettle goes on with a clatter louder than it needs to be. “Sit. You're dripping on my floor.”

Wren said she's never left the island, but in chapter I she sold eggs at the mainland market.
