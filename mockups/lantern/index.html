Chapter the third
Oil & Salt
in which a parcel is delivered, and a vote is postponed

The lamp room hummed as the great lens turned, throwing its slow gold arm across the black water. “You came back,” Wren said, not looking up from the brass she was polishing. “Most people only climb those stairs once.”

He was soaked through. Again. Of course he was.

I shake the rain off my coat and set the oilskin parcel on her workbench. “Your oil. The ferryman wouldn't come past the rocks, so I rowed it.”

Her hands went still on the cloth. “You rowed. In this.” She finally turned, and the lamplight caught the salt drying in her hair. “That's either very brave or very stupid, and I haven't decided which I'd prefer.”

She crossed to the stove before he could answer, and the kettle went on with a clatter that was louder than it needed to be.

I take the chair by the stove without arguing. “Brave. Definitely brave. I'll take the vote when you've had a cup.”

✦ ✦ ✦

She set two mugs down, and one of them was chipped, and she gave him the good one without seeming to think about it. “The vote,” she said, “is still out. But you can stay until the tide turns.”
